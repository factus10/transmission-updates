Why KKE Falls Short — The Five Dimensions

The core argument: KKE compresses five independent decisions into one column. The compression is invisible while audits are simple, then surfaces as a series of subtle bugs as audits get realistic.

This page enumerates the dimensions. The Patterns page shows where they collide with reality.

1. Scope — Is this page in this audit?

Not every page in the crawl is part of the audit's purview. Scope answers "should we be making decisions about this page at all?"

Value Meaning
InScope Actively being audited
OutOfCrawl Got crawled but isn't real content (search results, admin URLs, JSON feeds, sitemap fragments, infinite-scroll variants)
Deferred Valid content, not in this audit cycle (post-launch sections, next-phase work, future redesign scope)
OutOfAuthority Owned by another team/department/engagement, not yours to decide
ComplianceHold Can't be touched without legal review (regulated industries)
ThirdParty Aggregated content from another system (job listings, partner content, syndicated feeds)

KKE collapses all of these into "Exclude." That's why the Post-Launch problem keeps coming up — different reasons for being out-of-audit have different downstream implications, but they look identical in the data.

2. Content Fate — What happens to the content?

Independent of scope and URL. Answers "what should the content of this page become?"

Value Meaning KKE equivalent
Preserve Content stays as-is Keep
Improve Content is revised in place; retains identity Evolve
Replace Content is completely rewritten; new content, same role (none — usually shoehorned into Evolve)
Merge Content is absorbed by another page Consolidate
Split Content becomes 2+ pages (none — usually noted in free text)
Retire Content is removed Kill

Two of these are missing from KKE: Replace and Split.

  • Replace matters because "improve in place" implies iteration. A page that's stylistically dated but topically fine is Evolve. A page whose entire premise is wrong but whose URL is valuable is Replace. The content team needs to know which.
  • Split matters because the inverse of Consolidate happens too. One page doing three jobs (about the team + about services + about history) should become three pages.

3. URL Fate — What happens to the URL?

Mostly independent of content fate.

Value Meaning
Preserve URL stays exactly as it is
Move URL changes via nav reorganization (the page is Keep but lives at a new path)
Redirect URL retires; redirect points elsewhere
404 URL retires; no redirect (deliberate, e.g. for thin or noisy URLs)

Why this is independent:

  • A Keep page can get a new URL because the IA reorganized. Same content, different address.
  • A Kill page can have no redirect (intentional 404 for low-value or duplicate URLs).
  • A Consolidate page always redirects (to the target).
  • An Evolve page can keep its URL or move it.

KKE's single column tangles content fate and URL fate. Our audit tool fixes this implicitly with separate columns (KKE_Recommendation + Redirect_Destination + nav Computed Path), but the framework's vocabulary doesn't expose the distinction.

4. Process State — Where is this decision in the workflow?

Decisions move through a process. Where this row sits in that process is itself a piece of information.

Value Meaning
Unreviewed Auditor hasn't looked yet
InReview Examined; need more research before deciding
Decided Auditor is confident
PendingSME Sent for expert review
PendingStakeholder Sent for business decision
Resolved Final answer

KKE has implicit Unreviewed (blank) and Decided (filled in). The middle states are real — auditors absolutely encounter pages they need more info on — but there's no clean place to put them. They get parked in Notes, with the Flag bookmark, or in Tags.

The audit tool's Final_KKE column hints at this dimension: it's a separate column because the resolution is a separate moment from the initial recommendation. But the pattern stops at SME — it doesn't generalize to "needs research" or "pending stakeholder."

5. Authority — Whose decision is this?

The audit tool's column proliferation makes this dimension extremely visible:

Column Authority
KKE_Recommendation Auditor
SME_Decision Domain expert
GA_KKE_Recommendation Analytics-derived (PERCENTRANK on traffic + engagement)
AI_KKE AI pattern-match against rubric
Final_KKE The synthesis

Each authority has different weight on different pages:

  • A page with high traffic and strong engagement should weight Analytics heavily.
  • A page about regulated medical content should weight SME heavily.
  • A purely promotional page should weight Auditor + Stakeholder.

KKE-as-originally-conceived assumed one authority per page — the auditor. The audit tool has effectively built a parallel system of authority-stamped recommendations. Treat that as the framework, not as an exception.

Authority requires governance

Once you accept that an audit has multiple authorities producing recommendations, you also have to accept that authority-stamped vocabularies degrade without active management:

  • SMEs invent their own labels (Change: Consolidate, Change: Post-Launch, Evolve - Post)
  • Multiple auditors over multiple cycles introduce variants (Post Launch vs Post-Launch)
  • Imports from other tools bring legacy values

A framework that treats Authority as a real dimension has to provide vocabulary governance. Otherwise "multiple authorities" silently becomes "vocabulary drift," which silently becomes "the data column says one thing, the report says another, the migration team trusts neither."

What this analysis enables

Once you've named the five dimensions, you can ask cleaner questions:

  • "Why is this page hard to classify?" → which dimension is the source of the difficulty?
  • "Why don't auditors agree?" → are they disagreeing on Content Fate or URL Fate or Scope?
  • "Why does the migration team trust the audit data less than they should?" → is governance failing? Is Authority being flattened?

It also enables the proposed CAD framework, which decompresses these dimensions into separate columns.
